Shared by all three variants. The card is B's floating job card, now opaque. Ctrl+J or the chevron shrinks it into a pill next to F10; the F-keys slide left to make room. Ctrl+J or a click on the pill brings the card back. With several jobs the pill shows the count and the combined progress.

My recommendation

Build variant 3, and keep variant 1's coloured strip as the scrollbar. The symbol crumb in the header answers "where am I" without any panel, Ctrl+O reaches any symbol, mark or bookmark in two or three keystrokes, and the chip row keeps every mark one click or Alt+↓ away. Nothing takes width from the text when you are not using it, which is what the owner disliked about D's rail.

The strip costs almost nothing on top: B already draws ticks there, so adding mark colours and faint symbol ticks gives the spatial overview of variant 1 without its leader-line overlay. Skip variant 1's expanded map: it is the most novel, but it is the most expensive in GPUI (custom label layout and path drawing) and it hides the right edge of the text while open.

Variant 2 is the fallback if the owner wants marks and outline visible for long reading sessions; it is the cheapest to build and could later sit behind Ctrl+I as an extra on top of variant 3.

Copy: keep the card for the first two seconds of a job, then let Ctrl+J or the chevron park it in the pill. The pill fits a 1200 px window next to F10 with room to spare; below about 1100 px it should drop the job name first, then the time left.
